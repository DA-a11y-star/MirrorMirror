# Cyberpunk set: details

Moved out of CLAUDE.md (read this when working on the cyberpunk set, `web/props.js`, `web/roads.js` or `web/scenery.js`).

- **First slice, implemented: Cyberpunk city** (`?set=cyberpunk`; no in-app switching, host sync or epoch yet). Model "Cyberpunk City - #1" by born2012 (CC BY 4.0), 4.4 MB, 64k triangles, 30 meshes and materials, untextured, neon as emissive materials, no animation. It loads as scenery (`fitScenery` + `prepareScenery` in `web/scenery.js`: scaled, stood on y = 0, centred, transforms frozen, receives but casts no shadow), inside a night `environment`, with the blocks and spawns moved to its street (`workspace`). **Verified:** unit tests (placement, freezing, stats, asset validity and credit), and the e2e (loads, 64171 triangles, not in `blocks`, hovering and click-dragging on it targets and grabs nothing, no JS errors); **not** judged on a real display, and the headless fps is meaningless (software GL). Rendered cost at the original 0.3×: about 45 draw calls and 97k triangles; see the optimizations below for the 15× numbers.
- **What measuring the model showed, and the human-scale fix.** At 1× it is a miniature: 9 m wide, 3 m towers, streets about 1 m wide, with façades that are blank slabs. It is now scaled **15×** (towers 46 m, streets 15 m, 138 × 132 m), so you stand in a street under high-rises. A top-down occupancy grid of the scaled model (triangles between 0.6 and 2.5 m high) showed it is dense, with a few open plazas; **ground and spawn fix:** the model's streets are at 0.06 model units (0.9 m at 15×), not at its lowest point, so with the floor and the avatar clamp at y = 0 you stood 0.9 m inside the ground. `fitScenery` takes `ground` and lowers the model by `ground × scale` so the streets are at y = 0; the plain floor sits at `floorY` −0.2 so it never fights the ground. The first spawn search (a narrow height band measured from the wrong ground) was wrong and put users under or inside structures. Redone on the true ground: a column counts as blocked if any triangle rises above 0.9 m in it (so overpasses count), and the spawn is the nearest spot to the middle with a free 7 × 11 m box at street level: **`workspace` [14, −1]** (14 m east of centre; streets open 19 m north and 9 m south, buildings on both sides). The scan was a one-off script, not kept in the repo. `workspace` now offsets the blocks, **both spawns** (`main.js`) and the sun's shadow box. Avatars can fly through buildings (no collision). The 0.3× table-model view is gone.
- **Set extras (`web/props.js`, `web/roads.js`; config in `sets.js` `props`).** Three more CC BY models in `web/assets/cyberpunk/` (credits in `CREDITS.md`; a test checks each is a GLB with no required extensions, carries its licence and is credited).
  - **What the ground really is (measured, then drawn to check).** The model is not a street grid: building blocks stand in a lot of open space, and only some of it has ground geometry (about 35% of the area has none; the set's plain floor shows there). The ground has two levels, roads at 0.0603 model units and plazas at 0.0692 (13 cm higher), plus a few higher platforms. So `ground` is now 0.0692 (plazas at y = 0, roads 13 cm under it, and the painted surface covers both), and an earlier spawn search that assumed one level and counted the raised ground as walls was wrong. The roads are diagonal and curved, about 270 m of middle line in all, and narrow (the asphalt is 2 to 6 m wide after the pavement strip).
  - **`buildClearance`** (0.25 m cells; now also `low`, the lowest slice anything reaches in each cell, so overhead-only cells are known) and **`groundLevels`** (`scenery.js`) map buildings and ground levels; **`roadField`** adds the distance to the nearest building (blobs under 6 m² are ignored: lamp posts, signs; cells with only something overhead over road-level ground are not solid, so **an overpass no longer cuts the road under it**, which was the cause of the roads looking unconnected), the pavement strip `walk` (up to `sidewalk` 1.2 m but at most 30% of the open width within 3 m, so narrow roads keep their asphalt), asphalt = road-level ground at least `walk` from a building, and the distance to the asphalt's edge; **`connectRoads`** joins asphalt pieces (≥ 5 m²) by a Dijkstra search on a 1 m grid around buildings (clear of them by 2 m, off platforms, up to 70 m, then a minimum spanning tree), simplified to straight stretches, written as 3.2 m roads with pavement edges into a copy of the levels, after which the field is rebuilt (the first version had a 32-bit float cost array compared with doubles, which made the search skip valid nodes: fixed, test-covered); **`roadSkeleton`** is the asphalt's ridge (the middle line), smoothed, with each point's half-width, direction and straightness; **`findJunctions`** counts the separate roads leaving a circle of 5 m around each middle-line point (3 or more = a junction); **`paintRoads`** makes the RGBA texture (asphalt, kerb, white edge line, tiled pavement, dashed centre line on roads with half-width 2.5 m or more) and **`roadMesh`** lays it 2 cm above the ground with a depth offset, alpha-tested. Built once at load: about 300 ms for the whole set in Node (the road map, joining and painting), texture about 1500 × 1500. On the real city: 350 m of middle line, one connected network of about 2000 m² (4 links, 3 to 53 m) plus 3 tiny fragments walled off from it, 12 junctions.
  - **Cars:** `carParts` merges the car's 74 meshes into 10 looks (`mergeByLook`), scaled to 3.8 m with its nose toward +x (found from the headlights vs tail lights), the red body panels as one white part so each car takes its own colour from `setColorAt`; `Cars` draws every look as one `InstancedMesh` **per 50 m square of the city** (so the camera and far plane drop squares whole; 12 squares × 10 looks, only the visible squares are drawn). **`planRoadCars`** puts cars on the road middle line's lanes (right-hand traffic; a lane each way if the asphalt half-width is 2.5 m or more, otherwise one centred lane), wholly on asphalt (also nose and tail), 9–17 m behind the next car going the same way and a car length from any car. **`planAirCars`** hovers cars at 4–27 m on a jittered 14 m grid wherever the clearance map says the footprint is open air and within 25 m of a building. Both outside 10 m of the blocks. **Cars now fill the whole city** (the earlier "only the first view" limit was dropped at the user's request: cars were all on one edge); planned from the set name's seed and the model alone, so both laptops get the same cars. Counts (the user asked for more hovering than driving): 20 road cars and 40 hovering ones, the same on both laptops.
  - **Street lamps are shared, grabbable objects.** `planLamps` picks points on the pavement 0.65 m past the asphalt's edge, about 17–24 m apart, within 70 m of the blocks and 5 m clear of them, nearest first, from a fixed seed (so both laptops get the same lamps in the same order). `lampSpec(lamp, i, origin)` makes each an assembly (base, 9 m pole, 2.6 m arm over the road, glowing lamp head; 9.8 m tall) with id `lamp-<n>`, **positioned relative to the blocks group** (`origin` = the workspace): the first version used world coordinates inside the offset `blocks` group, so the lamps stood 14 m from where planned, away from the roads and the user's view, which is why they could not be grabbed (**unit**: a lamp's base lands at its planned world position inside a group at the workspace). `main.js` adds them with `Objects.add(..., {place: false, remember: false, notify: false})` and `SceneSync.adopt(root)` (registers an assembly built locally so the host's scenes carry it and holds on it stream). Assembly parts gained an optional `emissive` colour (`object-protocol.js`, `objects.js`; `Interaction` restores it after highlights). They count toward `MAX_OBJECTS` (64) and are not undoable. **Limits:** both laptops must open the same set, and a guest that builds them before the host does has them removed by the host's first scene (which lacks them) and rebuilt from the host's next full scene (at most 2 s later). **Not yet tried with a real hand or gaze:** targeting is by each object's screen outline, so a 9.8 m pole has a large one.
  - **Floating junction lights (`JunctionLights`).** A hovering disc 7.5 m up over each junction (those with open air at that height), a glowing underside and a faint additive cone of light down to the road, in rotating pink, cyan and amber; three instanced meshes in all, the discs bob. Decoration only (not shared or grabbable).
  - **Eve and Wall-E:** `fitFigure` scales Eve to 1.5 m, hovering, emissive eyes; `buildWallE` (primitives, 1.1 m). Both stand 2 m west of the blocks facing them.
  - **Airship:** `airshipMesh` merges its 305 meshes into 9 (130k triangles, fog off); `airshipAt` (pure) flies a level circle of 95 m around the workspace at 50 m, one lap per 300 s (about 2 m/s), nose along the tangent. The circle lies mostly outside the city's footprint and above its tallest building (46 m), so it never meets a building; it is 107 m from the spawn, just inside the 120 m far plane, so a laptop far from the spawn can lose it behind the far plane. Its heading assumes the model's nose is +x (unchecked by eye): `yawOffset` flips it. It replaces the earlier view-leash (`stepAirship`, removed).
  - **Verified:** **unit** (`tests/props.test.mjs`: ground levels, road field, skeleton, painting, lanes and gaps, air-car clearance, lamp placement and the assembly (valid, glowing, grounded), `adopt`, view, airship circle and heading, merging, car parts and instancing; mutation-checked for the lane side, the pavement strip, the overpass handling, the cost array type, the lamp coordinates, the car squares, junction detection, and the circle, but **not** for the minimum-spanning-tree filter (the test has only two pieces), a car's centre on asphalt, or lamps off asphalt) plus Node dry runs on the real assets (top-down maps of the ground levels, roads, links, junctions, cars and lamps were drawn and checked by eye). **Never rendered**: no browser check of how any of it looks, of the airship's direction, or of the frame rate.
- **Optimizations (cyberpunk, `web/scenery.js` + `sets.js`).** (1) `chunkScenery` splits the model's 30 per-material meshes (each as big as the city, so never cullable) into `chunk`-metre cells (64 m) with the transform baked in, so frustum culling works. (2) `camera.far` = fog far + 10 (120 m), so cells in the haze are culled; fog is 25–110 m. (3) Scenery casts and receives no shadows (`shadows` in the set). (4) Frozen matrices. (5) The 60 m plain floor is enlarged to 400 m (`environment.floorSize`). (6) `Locomotion` takes `bound` and `ceiling` per set (90 m and 60 m here) instead of the 20 m and 12 m defaults. Measured once in headless software GL (no fps): spawn view 17–113 draw calls (cell size 24 m: up to 297, 40 m: 179, 100 m: 104), 37k triangles from 45 m up with 69 calls. **unit** (`chunkScenery` keeps triangles and placement, culls past the far plane; mutation-checked) only; the e2e's city assertions were edited (mesh count `>= 30`, blocks at the set's workspace, scenery not moved) but **not re-run**, and none of this is judged on a real display. Ideas not done: merge materials that differ only by colour (30 materials make the draw-call floor), distance LOD or a coarser far ring, instancing repeated props, a collision grid.
